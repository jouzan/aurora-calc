Terms used on this page

Two of these are mine, not the file's

Cluster vs. instance

The file's unit of measurement

Each of the 110 rows is one Aurora cluster — one shared storage volume, one writer, and one to four read replicas. Storage belongs to the cluster, not to any instance, so the petabyte is counted once, not once per replica.

"Live shards"

My label, not a field in the export

Shorthand for the 84 clusters whose names do not contain cmek — the ones still taking production traffic and growing. Used to separate real growth from the teardown that is cancelling it out in the headline number.

CMEK

Inferred from the naming convention

Customer-managed encryption key — clusters encrypted with a key you control in KMS rather than the AWS-owned default. The export never says this. I read it off the *-cmek-* names; if that prefix means something else in your estate, reread that section as "the 26 clusters named cmek."

The growth number is two numbers

Capacity forecast

Summing the growth column gives a comfortable-looking figure. It is the sum of a fleet expanding hard and a fleet being deleted, and only one of those can continue.

What to plan against

Budget for the post-migration rate, not the current one

Once the CMEK teardown completes — about two months out — the drain that is masking growth disappears and the fleet's net rate snaps up to the live-shard rate. Twelve-month projections differ by well over 300 TB depending on which number you use.

Where the capacity actually sits

Distribution by volume band

This is not a smooth distribution. Forty-four clusters cluster tightly in the 15–20 TB band and carry two thirds of all stored data, which points to a deliberate shard-size target somewhere around 18 TB. At the other end, 23 clusters hold under 1 TB each and contribute almost nothing.

Largest cluster , against Aurora's 128 TiB per-cluster ceiling — no cluster is within an order of magnitude of the hard limit, so shard splits are a data-distribution decision here, not a capacity one.

Compute is bought in one size

Writer instance mix

The 110 clusters run 325 instances in us-east-1. Seventy-seven of the writers are the same instance type, regardless of whether the cluster holds 20 TB or 70 MB. Provisioning is following a template rather than the workload.

Standard or I/O-Optimized

Storage & I/O economics

Aurora Standard bills storage and I/O separately; I/O-Optimized folds I/O into a higher storage and instance rate. The switch pays off above roughly 0.625 million I/O per GB per month on the storage side alone. This fleet is not close.

Verdict

Stay on Aurora Standard

The fleet is storage-heavy and I/O-light: a petabyte of mostly-cold volume generating well under half a million I/O per GB. Moving to I/O-Optimized would raise the storage line by more than it removes from the I/O line, before the ~30% compute premium is even counted.

Notes and assumptions
  1. Every figure is derived directly from grid_db_aurora_clusters.csv. Storage is the latest VolumeBytesUsed reading; growth is the file's own 60-day linear fit, extrapolated forward unchanged.
  2. "CMEK" is inferred from the cluster naming convention (*-cmek-*), which the file does not otherwise label. Classes are assigned here: near-idle is ≤ 50M I/O per month, hot is a non-CMEK cluster growing ≥ 10 GB/day.
  3. Cost figures are rough on-demand estimates at us-east-1 list rates: $0.13 per vCPU-hour for r6g Aurora MySQL compute (from the $0.26/hour db.r6g.large rate), $0.10 per GB-month Standard storage, $0.20 per million I/O, $0.225 per GB-month I/O-Optimized storage. Reserved instances, savings plans, backups, snapshots, cross-region replication and data transfer are not included, and replica compute is not priced because the export does not list replica instance types. Treat these as order-of-magnitude, not as a bill.
  4. One cluster, prod-idbroker-cluster, runs engine version 8.0.3.08.2 while the other 109 run 8.0.3.10.3 — worth reconciling with your patch baseline.
  5. Replica counts come from how many replica-n read-I/O columns are populated. A replica doing no reads is recorded as 0 rather than N/A (23 clusters show exactly that), so blank really does mean "no such replica" and the count of 215 is sound. Replica instance types are not in the export.
